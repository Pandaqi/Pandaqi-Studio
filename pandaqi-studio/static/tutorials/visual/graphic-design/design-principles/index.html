Design Principles

This is the third section of the course on Graphic Design. It is about design principles.

The first two sections were about elements and their properties. Guess what this last section does? It combines them.

Design principles are ideas about how multiple elements can best be combined into the final design. It’s by far the longest section of the course, because it’s also the most important and varied concept.

It’s easy to explain a rectangle. It’s easy to place one on a page. It’s less straightforward when a design needs hundreds of rectangles and you still need to make it look clear and good.

Hierarchy

We’ve discussed the fundamental visual elements and their properties. Let’s move on to the bigger picture: placing all the elements together within the same design—without turning it into complete chaos.

Grouping

Grouping is the first step for creating hierarchy. It serves to both combine and separate. As a process of combining, grouping transforms elements into larger elements. As a process of separating, grouping serves to break down large and complex objects into smaller, simpler ones.
Design Principles Graphic Design

Figure-Ground

The first step towards ranking elements in your design, is establishing the figure-ground relationship. I’ve referred to the distinction between foreground and background several times already. The term figure-ground is just design language for the same thing.
Design Principles Graphic Design

Layering

Great! You’ve now established the figure-ground relationship. It’s time to establish the hierarchy between smaller groups of elements. I think it’s best to do this in different levels or layers.
Design Principles Graphic Design

Grid

So far, you can establish figure-ground (foreground and background). Within those, you can create layers for each general “level” of the hierarchy. Now let’s go one step further: inside a layer, you often want to use one grid.
Design Principles Graphic Design

Contrast

Contrast allows our eyes to see. Without it, everything would be the same, and we wouldn’t be able to distinguish different objects or elements in a design.
Design Principles Graphic Design

Alignment

Following contrast, alignment is the strongest principle. It does the same as contrast, but reversed and more general.
Design Principles Graphic Design

Proximity

Together with alignment, proximity is the second strongest design principle. Elements that are close together are perceived to be more related than elements that are farther apart.

Repetition

We’ve already seen the importance of repetition on a small scale, with patterns and alignment. Now we can take it one step further. Repetition within a design could also just be called being consistent.
